GridLoad tells you at a glance whether now is a good time to run household appliances like the washing machine, dishwasher or EV charger. It reads your utility's dynamic electricity price and shows one colour:

<ul>
<li><b>Green, good time:</b> electricity is cheap, run your appliances now.</li>
<li><b>Orange, fair time:</b> average price, only run what you need.</li>
<li><b>Red, bad time:</b> electricity is expensive, wait if you can.</li>
</ul>

The price now is compared with the next 24 hours, so red means a cheaper time is coming, and the app shows when the next good time starts. Cheap times are when the grid has the most to spare, for example at midday when solar power peaks. Shifting load to those times lowers your bill and helps the grid.

<b>Regions:</b> in Switzerland the price comes from your local utility's dynamic tariff: Central Switzerland (CKW), Canton of Zurich (EKZ), Einsiedeln (EKZ Einsiedeln), Fribourg and Neuchâtel (Groupe E), Northwestern Switzerland (Primeo Energie), Olten area (AVAG) and Gretzenbach (ELAG). In Austria, Belgium (Flanders, and Wallonia and Brussels), Germany, Liechtenstein, Luxembourg and the Netherlands it's the day-ahead market price (Bundesnetzagentur | SMARD.de, via energy-charts.info, CC BY 4.0); enter your supplier's add-on to see your own price.

<b>whatwatt:</b> with a whatwatt Go smart meter reader on your home Wi-Fi (with its Plus licence), GridLoad also shows what your home draws right now and what that costs per hour.

<b>Peak load:</b> some tariffs also charge for the month's highest 15-minute average (for example CKW's Home dynamic, Flanders, and Austria from 2027). With a whatwatt, GridLoad shows this quarter hour's projected draw against a limit (the month's highest, raised to what your biggest appliance needs or to your own goal) and how many kW are still free, and warns with a red bar and a vibration when it reaches the limit. A history shows each day's highest quarter hour this month; tap it for a day's quarter hours and last month. A small recorder script that GridLoad installs on the whatwatt saves every quarter hour on its SD card, also while the app is closed, and the app tells you exactly when it stops.

<b>Appliances:</b> measure each appliance once with the whatwatt, and GridLoad says per appliance whether to switch it on now or wait, and until when: so it doesn't reach the limit, or, for a dishwasher or washing machine, because a later start is clearly cheaper.

<b>Privacy:</b> no account, no ads, no tracking. Internet access loads today's and tomorrow's prices from the utility or Energy-Charts. On Android 17 and later, the local network permission lets the app read your whatwatt; it's asked for only if you connect one.
