GridLoad tells you at a glance whether now is a good time to run household appliances like the washing machine, dishwasher or EV charger. It reads your utility's dynamic electricity price and shows one colour:

<ul>
<li><b>Green, good time:</b> electricity is cheap, run your appliances now.</li>
<li><b>Orange, fair time:</b> average price, only run what you need.</li>
<li><b>Red, bad time:</b> electricity is expensive, wait if you can.</li>
</ul>

The price now is compared with the next 24 hours, so red means a cheaper time is coming, and the app shows when the next good time starts. Cheap times are when the grid has the most to spare, for example at midday when solar power peaks. Shifting load to those times lowers your bill and helps the grid.

<b>Regions:</b> the price comes from your local utility, so GridLoad only works where a utility publishes a dynamic tariff. Supported: Central Switzerland (CKW), Canton of Zurich (EKZ), Einsiedeln (EKZ Einsiedeln), Fribourg and Neuchâtel (Groupe E), Northwestern Switzerland (Primeo Energie), Olten area (AVAG) and Gretzenbach (ELAG).

<b>whatwatt:</b> with a whatwatt Go smart meter reader on your home Wi-Fi (with its Plus licence), GridLoad also shows what your home draws right now and what that costs per hour.

<b>Peak load mode:</b> some tariffs also charge for the month's highest 15-minute average (for example CKW's Home dynamic). This mode is being rebuilt on live readings from a whatwatt smart meter reader; until then it shows the spot price.

<b>Privacy:</b> no account, no ads, no tracking. Internet access loads today's and tomorrow's prices from the utility. On Android 17 and later, the local network permission lets the app read your whatwatt; it's asked for only if you connect one.
